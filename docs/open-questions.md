| OQ-13 | Incremental or generational collection, and a write-barrier API, for the chosen GC pattern | S1's Pattern B pauses are stop-the-world: about 4 ms at 130k live nodes and 15 ms at 333k objects. | OQ-14 | Root-stack design for Pattern B: can rooting cost at most 1 ns per root and per read? | Rooting dominates B's overhead in code that holds many handles across possible collections: dispatch takes 2.06× A's time. Measured unit costs are 2.05 ns per root and 1.58 ns per read (S1 condition C1) | A prototype measured with `s1-probe roots` and the dispatch suite; an interpreter-shaped benchmark | Step 4 | Open, raised by S1. **Spike S3 designed** (2026-10-17, [Step 4 tracker](research/step-4-architecture-proposal.md) §10): a root stack without borrow tracking against a 1 ns target. **Authorized at Gate B** (2026-10-17). **Answered by spike S3** (2026-10-18, [S3 results](research/s3-results.md)): without borrow tracking, a read costs 0.51 ns and a frame 0.75 ns; a push costs 0.56 ns back to back, but 2.03 ns by S1's method, which every stack shares. Rooting takes about 1% of a callback-heavy interpreter workload. **Resolved** (2026-10-18) with [ADR-0008](adr/0008-script-agents-and-heap.md), accepted at Gate C after S3's addendum: C1's stack is `SliceStack`, with no `unsafe`. It reads in 0.53 ns, and by S1's method pushes in about 2.1 ns, as every stack does; that figure depends on how the code around the push compiles ([S3 results](research/s3-results.md) §3) |
