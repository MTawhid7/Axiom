| OQ-13 | Incremental or generational collection, and a write-barrier API, for the chosen GC pattern | S1's Pattern B pauses are stop-the-world: about 4 ms at 130k live nodes and 15 ms at 333k objects. Its plain field writes cannot support incremental marking (S1 conditions C3, C4) | ADR-0003; a pause budget (Step 6 performance section); a barrier prototype, with S1-Q2's ceremony recounted | Step 4, before the JS object model is fixed | **Design resolved** (2026-10-18, with [ADR-0008](adr/0008-script-agents-and-heap.md) SH9–SH10, accepted at Step 4's Gate C; S3-Q5 measures the barrier's cost; on 2026-10-18 it found setters free without a barrier, and card marking at about 3% on mutation-heavy work): generated setters for every handle-valued field are the barrier point. The collector stays stop-the-world for now. Incremental marking in idle periods and a generational nursery come later, in the order corpus measurements demand. Step 6 sets the pause budget |
| OQ-14 | Root-stack design for Pattern B: can rooting cost at most 1 ns per root and per read? | Rooting dominates B's overhead in code that holds many handles across possible collections: dispatch takes 2.06× A's time. Measured unit costs are 2.05 ns per root and 1.58 ns per read (S1 condition C1) | A prototype measured with `s1-probe roots` and the dispatch suite; an interpreter-shaped benchmark | Step 4 | Open, raised by S1. **Spike S3 designed** (2026-10-17, [Step 4 tracker](research/step-4-architecture-proposal.md) §10): a root stack without borrow tracking against a 1 ns target. **Authorized at Gate B** (2026-10-17). **Answered by spike S3** (2026-10-18, [S3 results](research/s3-results.md)): without borrow tracking, a read costs 0.51 ns and a frame 0.75 ns; a push costs 0.56 ns back to back, but 2.03 ns by S1's method, which every stack shares. Rooting takes about 1% of a callback-heavy interpreter workload. **Resolved** (2026-10-18) with [ADR-0008](adr/0008-script-agents-and-heap.md), accepted at Gate C after S3's addendum: C1's stack is `SliceStack`, with no `unsafe`. It reads in 0.53 ns, and by S1's method pushes in about 2.1 ns, as every stack does; that figure depends on how the code around the push compiles ([S3 results](research/s3-results.md) §3) |

| OQ-17 | IPC transport: Mach messages or Unix-domain socket pairs? | Latency, handle passing (IOSurfaces need Mach ports), peer identity, complexity | Spike S4-Q3 measurements; Step 4 design | Step 4 | Open, raised by the [process model](security/process-model.md) (Step 3). **S4 evidence (2026-10-17) favours Mach messages:** 3.75 against 4.92 µs per 64-byte round trip; port rights for IOSurfaces and memory; the kernel's audit trailer for peer identity. 1 MiB transfers are comparable once socket buffers are enlarged from 8 KiB ([S4 results](research/s4-results.md) §4). **Resolved 2026-10-17 (Gate B):** [ADR-0005](adr/0005-ipc-transport-and-interfaces.md) accepted: Mach messages, the only transport that carries every handle Axiom needs, IOSurfaces included, with an audit token on every message |
